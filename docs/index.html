Do free downloads turn into customers?

I've run a music production business since 2018, licensing my instrumentals to artists online. Like most producers, I give some away as free downloads to build an email list. I joined my sales and email data to find out whether that actually works.

02The question

Is a free download worth giving away?

Every free download is a track someone didn't pay for. In return, the downloader is added to my email list automatically. The trade only makes sense if some of them come back and buy.

  1. What share of free downloaders become paying customers?
  2. How long do they take to buy?
  3. Are buyers who stay on the list worth more than buyers who don't?

03The data

Two messy exports

An export of every order from January 2020 to September 2026, and an export of my current email list. I cleaned both in Python with pandas, loaded them into SQLite, and answered every question in SQL.

Block layout

Each order came out as a block of rows: the order, then a row per extra track and per collaborator's share.

Parsed line by line into orders, items and payment splits.

Broken columns

Track titles containing commas had spilled into the next column.

Detected by field count and rejoined.

Revenue splits

Most sales are split with collaborators, so the sale price isn't what I earned.

Kept every party's share separately and anonymised every collaborator.

Free checkouts

Some items went through checkout at a price of zero.

Flagged so they never count as purchases.

The detail that changed the answer

Two systems, two clocks

+4:00 EDT+5:00 EST

When I first joined the files, most buyers seemed to subscribe exactly four or five hours before buying. That's the gap between US Eastern time and UTC: the email platform appears to record Eastern time without labelling it. With the clocks aligned, those buyers had joined the list at checkout, seconds before paying. Without the fix they'd have counted as free downloaders who converted, and the headline result would have been several times too high.

Decisions behind the numbers

  • The one-hour rule. Buyers are added to the list at checkout, so a conversion only counts when the first purchase comes more than an hour after joining.
  • Medians over averages. A few buyers took most of a year, which would drag an average. The median shows the typical wait.
  • Imported contacts left out of timing. Their join date is the day I uploaded them, so it says nothing about when they found me.
  • Fisher's exact test for comparisons. The groups are small and repeat purchases are rare, which is where this test is reliable.
  • Privacy by design. Every query returns a rate, share, median or index. Small groups are grouped or rounded so no total can be worked out.

Privacy: customer emails are replaced with salted one-way hashes, so the files can be joined without anyone being identifiable. The data stays on my computer, and shares based on small groups are rounded so the numbers behind them can't be worked out.

-- sql/00_views.sql
-- Shared building blocks for every analysis query.
-- A "paid purchase" is an item with a sale price above zero, in an order
-- that is completed or has no recorded status. The one pending order is excluded.

DROP VIEW IF EXISTS paid_items;
CREATE VIEW paid_items AS
SELECT
    o.order_id,
    o.customer_id,
    o.order_time_utc,
    o.channel,
    i.item_no,
    i.track_title,
    i.list_price,
    i.sale_price
FROM orders AS o
JOIN order_items AS i USING (order_id)
WHERE o.status <> 'pending'
  AND i.sale_price > 0;


DROP VIEW IF EXISTS paid_orders;
CREATE VIEW paid_orders AS
SELECT
    order_id,
    customer_id,
    MIN(order_time_utc) AS order_time_utc,
    MIN(channel)        AS channel,
    SUM(sale_price)     AS order_value,
    COUNT(*)            AS items
FROM paid_items
GROUP BY order_id, customer_id;


-- One row per paying customer.
DROP VIEW IF EXISTS buyers;
CREATE VIEW buyers AS
SELECT
    customer_id,
    MIN(order_time_utc) AS first_paid_time,
    COUNT(*)            AS paid_orders,
    SUM(order_value)    AS total_spend
FROM paid_orders
GROUP BY customer_id;


-- How each buyer relates to the email list.
--   joined_list_first   : added automatically by the sales platform (almost always a free download) more than an hour before first paying
--   joined_at_checkout  : added automatically at the moment of their first purchase
--   file_import         : added from a spreadsheet in 2021, so the join date is not meaningful
--   not_on_list         : not currently subscribed
DROP VIEW IF EXISTS buyer_paths;
CREATE VIEW buyer_paths AS
SELECT
    b.customer_id,
    b.first_paid_time,
    b.paid_orders,
    b.total_spend,
    s.optin_time_utc,
    julianday(b.first_paid_time) - julianday(s.optin_time_utc) AS days_to_first_purchase,
    CASE
        WHEN s.customer_id IS NULL THEN 'not_on_list'
        WHEN s.source = 'file_import' THEN 'file_import'
        WHEN julianday(b.first_paid_time) - julianday(s.optin_time_utc) > 1.0 / 24 THEN 'joined_list_first'
        ELSE 'joined_at_checkout'
    END AS path
FROM buyers AS b
LEFT JOIN subscribers AS s USING (customer_id);

-- sql/01_free_download_conversion.sql
-- Q1. What share of free downloaders go on to buy a paid licence?
-- Free downloaders: subscribers the sales platform added after a free download.
-- Converted: their first paid purchase came more than an hour after they joined.
-- (Within the hour means they joined by buying, not by downloading.)
-- Returns a rate only, so no audience or sales volumes are exposed.

WITH free_downloaders AS (
    SELECT
        f.customer_id,
        b.first_paid_time IS NOT NULL
            AND julianday(b.first_paid_time) - julianday(f.optin_time_utc) > 1.0 / 24 AS converted
    FROM subscribers AS f
    LEFT JOIN buyers AS b USING (customer_id)
    WHERE f.source = 'auto_signup'
      AND f.tag_free_download = 1
)
SELECT
    ROUND(100.0 * AVG(converted), 1)  AS conversion_pct,
    ROUND(1.0 / AVG(converted), 0)    AS one_in
FROM free_downloaders;

-- sql/02_time_to_convert.sql
-- Q2. For free downloaders who converted, how long did it take?
-- Returns the share in each time band, rounded to whole percentages.

WITH converted AS (
    SELECT julianday(b.first_paid_time) - julianday(s.optin_time_utc) AS days
    FROM subscribers AS s
    JOIN buyers AS b USING (customer_id)
    WHERE s.source = 'auto_signup'
      AND s.tag_free_download = 1
      AND julianday(b.first_paid_time) - julianday(s.optin_time_utc) > 1.0 / 24
)
SELECT
    CASE
        WHEN days <= 7   THEN '1. Within a week'
        WHEN days <= 30  THEN '2. 1 to 4 weeks'
        WHEN days <= 90  THEN '3. 1 to 3 months'
        ELSE                  '4. 3 to 12 months'
    END                                                   AS time_to_convert,
    100.0 * COUNT(*) / SUM(COUNT(*)) OVER ()              AS pct
FROM converted
GROUP BY 1
ORDER BY 1;

-- sql/02b_median_days.sql
-- Q2b. Median days from free download to first purchase.

WITH converted AS (
    SELECT julianday(b.first_paid_time) - julianday(s.optin_time_utc) AS days
    FROM subscribers AS s
    JOIN buyers AS b USING (customer_id)
    WHERE s.source = 'auto_signup'
      AND s.tag_free_download = 1
      AND julianday(b.first_paid_time) - julianday(s.optin_time_utc) > 1.0 / 24
),
ranked AS (
    SELECT days,
           ROW_NUMBER() OVER (ORDER BY days) AS rn,
           COUNT(*) OVER ()                  AS n
    FROM converted
)
SELECT ROUND(AVG(days), 0) AS median_days
FROM ranked
WHERE rn IN ((n + 1) / 2, (n + 2) / 2);

-- sql/03_repeat_rate_by_path.sql
-- Q3. Do buyers on the list come back more often?
-- Limited to buyers whose first purchase came after the list started (May 2021),
-- so every buyer had the chance to be on it. File imports are excluded:
-- their join date is the day I uploaded them, not when they found me.

SELECT
    CASE WHEN path = 'not_on_list' THEN 'Not on the list' ELSE 'On the list' END AS list_status,
    ROUND(100.0 * AVG(paid_orders > 1), 1)            AS repeat_pct
FROM buyer_paths
WHERE first_paid_time >= '2021-05-05'
  AND path <> 'file_import'
GROUP BY 1
ORDER BY 1 DESC;

-- sql/04_list_share_by_period.sql
-- Q4. Where do new buyers come from: the peak years against recent years.
-- Share of first-time buyers who had joined the list before buying.
-- Years are grouped into two periods because single recent years are small.

SELECT
    CASE WHEN first_paid_time < '2024-01-01' THEN '1. 2021 to 2023' ELSE '2. 2024 to 2026' END AS period,
    ROUND(100.0 * AVG(path = 'joined_list_first'), 0)                                     AS joined_list_first_pct
FROM buyer_paths
WHERE first_paid_time >= '2021-05-05'
GROUP BY 1
ORDER BY 1;

05What I'd change

Follow up for months

The list works, but slowly. Most of the value arrives long after a welcome email would have been forgotten.

  1. Email free downloaders for a year

    About 70% of conversions came after the first week. Emails at one week, one month, three months and six months match when people actually buy. If they turned one more downloader in every 200 into a customer, the list would bring in about 31% more new customers.

  2. Send new releases to past buyers first

    Buyers on the list are five times as likely to buy again, and buyers are added to it at checkout. They're the warmest audience I have.

  3. Fix the sync, then test it

    Check the connection between the store and the email list so no sign-ups are lost again. Then send the follow-ups to a random half of new downloaders and compare. At a 1.6% baseline, track opens and clicks early and judge purchases after six months.

-- sql/06_channel_mix_by_year.sql
-- Q6. Share of paid orders through my own storefront versus the platform's public marketplace.

SELECT
    CASE WHEN order_time_utc < '2024-01-01' THEN strftime('%Y', order_time_utc) ELSE '2024 to 2026' END AS period,
    ROUND(100.0 * AVG(channel = 'own_store'), 0)     AS own_storefront_pct,
    ROUND(100.0 * AVG(channel = 'marketplace'), 0)  AS marketplace_pct
FROM paid_orders
GROUP BY 1
ORDER BY 1;

-- sql/08_catalogue_concentration.sql
-- Q8. How concentrated are sales across the catalogue?

WITH per_track AS (
    SELECT track_title, COUNT(*) AS sales
    FROM paid_items
    GROUP BY track_title
),
ranked AS (
    SELECT sales, ROW_NUMBER() OVER (ORDER BY sales DESC) AS rnk
    FROM per_track
)
SELECT
    ROUND(100.0 * SUM(CASE WHEN rnk = 1   THEN sales END) / SUM(sales), 0) AS top_track_pct,
    ROUND(100.0 * SUM(CASE WHEN rnk <= 10 THEN sales END) / SUM(sales), 0) AS top_10_pct,
    ROUND(100.0 * SUM(sales = 1) / COUNT(*), 0)                            AS sold_once_pct
FROM ranked;

-- sql/09_collaboration.sql
-- Q9. How much of the catalogue is co-produced?

WITH item_parties AS (
    SELECT order_id, item_no, SUM(party <> 'me') AS collaborators
    FROM payment_splits
    GROUP BY order_id, item_no
)
SELECT ROUND(100.0 * AVG(collaborators > 0), 0) AS co_produced_pct
FROM item_parties;

07Limits

What the data can't tell me

  • A gap in 2025.Almost nobody joined the list during 2025, against steady sign-ups every other year, and sign-ups pick up again in 2026. The most likely cause is that the automatic sync between my store and the email list stopped for most of that year, so people from that period are missing from the list.
  • Current subscribers only.Anyone who bought and then unsubscribed counts as never having been on the list.
  • One download per person.A free download is visible only through its tag and the date the person first joined. Later downloads aren't recorded.
  • Imported contacts.Contacts imported from a spreadsheet in 2021 have their upload date as their join date, so they're left out of the timing and repeat questions.
  • Correlation, not cause.Recent years are small. The test in recommendation 3 is how I'd find out what causes what.
